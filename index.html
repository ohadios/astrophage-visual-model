Model Notes

A toy model of the Astrophage lifecycle from Project Hail Mary (Andy Weir). It is built to be illustrative and faithful to the book's framing — not a rigorous astrophysical simulation. Here's what I treat as canon versus what I invented to make it run.

✓ Facts (from the book & author)

  • Astrophage is a single-celled organism ~10 µm across with an Earth-like cell structure (DNA, ATP). It holds a constant internal temperature of 96.415 °C — absorbing excess heat as stored energy, and burning reserves to stay warm in the cold of space.
  • It "charges" at the Sun, converting stellar energy into mass near the E=mc² limit. A single cell can absorb about 1.5 MJ before it is "enriched" (can store no more) — per Dimitri Komorov's laser tests in the book.
  • Once enriched, it departs straight away from the solar pole, then turns sharply toward Venus — that path is the Petrova line. It responds to the Sun's magnetic field only at the very start of the trip, then locks onto Venus by its CO₂ spectral signature.
  • It reproduces by cell division in Venus's CO₂-rich upper atmosphere; parent and child both return to the Sun, so one migration cycle doubles the population.
  • It propels itself by emitting infrared at the Petrova wavelength, 25.984 µm — the light that forms the Petrova line. (The 4.26 µm and 18.31 µm bands are the CO₂ emission peaks it homes on, and that the ship's spin drive uses to herd it — not its propulsion wavelength.)
  • It dims its host star and, for reasons the book explicitly leaves unexplained, stops at about 10% dimming. A 10% drop is enough to trigger a terminal ice age within decades (worst in-book projection: 3.5 billion dead in ~19 years).
  • The Sun's fusion is untouched; the dimming is an "umbrella" effect — a vast shroud of cells absorbs light before it escapes to space.
  • Tau Ceti resisted infection because its planet (Adrian) hosts a predator microbe, Taumoeba, that keeps astrophage in check. Taumoeba requires CO₂, so it lives in the target planet's atmosphere and can't follow astrophage to the Sun; the Adrian strain dies at Venus's nitrogen levels (hence the bred "Taumoeba-82.5").
  • In spore form it can cross up to ~8 light-years between stars, drawn to starlight.
  • Per Weir, the physics is "mostly real" except the quantum-level detail of energy storage (neutrinos / pair production).

⚙ Assumptions (mine, to make it run)

  • 8-day charge time at the Sun — a working figure. I did not find a specific charge duration stated in the book. (Gemini also cites "~8 days," but I treat it as unverified.)
  • I render the polar departure as a climb to the north pole to "survey" the planets for the most CO₂. The straight-out-from-the-solar-pole path is canon; the "rises to get a better view" motivation and choosing the north pole specifically are my embellishment.
  • "Unobstructed view" crowd-avoidance — astrophage spread to less-crowded directions; my addition.
  • The big one — growth rate. Canon says each migration cycle doubles the population (parent + child both return) on a ~15-day cycle — which would shroud the Sun in months — yet canon also says ~30 years to 10% dimming. The book is internally in tension here. I reconcile it by imposing a slow logistic curve (~1.4% net per cycle, ~2.1-year doubling) tuned so the colony climbs from ~0.01% dimming at detection to the 10% ceiling in ~30 years — rather than deriving that rate. A real limiter — self-shading (a dense colony starving its own recruits of light), CO₂-limited breeding, or migration mortality — would make it emergent, and self-shading could even explain the unexplained 10% ceiling.
  • The carrying-capacity ceiling is a stand-in for that unknown limiter.
  • The head-count (carrying capacity ~3×10¹⁶) is illustrative only; the true number needed to physically block 10% of the Sun is vastly larger. (The book's one hard figure is per-cell: ~1.5 MJ when enriched.)
  • The clock starts at first detection. In the book, Dr. Irina Petrova spots the infrared Petrova line first; a solar probe then measures the Sun ~0.01% dimmer. I map that to ~0.1% of peak saturation (~30 trillion cells), and tune the growth so the 10% threshold arrives ~30 years later — matching the book. (Adjustable via the starting-population slider.)
  • Orbits are modeled as circular and coplanar; real Venus/Earth orbits are slightly eccentric and inclined.
  • Sun rotation is a uniform ~25 days (real equatorial value); no differential rotation.
  • Near-light-speed transits are slowed so they're visible.
  • Taumoeba predator-prey constants are tuned for a watchable crash/recovery, not measured; the nitrogen-tolerance failure mode is not modeled.
  • On-screen particles are a representative visual sample, decoupled from the true population count.
